# Lesson 9: Making your usage last

## The idea

Your Claude plan has a usage limit. What uses it up: long chats (Claude rereads the whole chat every message), big models at high effort, and reading lots of files. Ways to make it last:

1. **New chat for each bigger task.** Nothing is lost if your notes and CLAUDE.md are set up. Start each one with your session start prompt.
2. **Match the model and effort to the task** (lesson 6).
3. **Point Claude at the right note or file.** "Read notes/Pets.md first" beats letting it search the whole project.
4. **Only ask for what you'll use.** No "and also make it look nice" unless you mean it.
5. **Stop when it works.** Polish can be its own small task later.
6. **Turn routines into skills** (lesson 10), so you don't re-explain the same steps every time.

## Why

A 3-hour chat about five different things costs much more than five short chats, and it gets worse at the end because the early details get summarized away.

## From Battle for Pets

Our CLAUDE.md has a section called "Keep work simple and efficient": build only what's asked, choose the simplest approach, keep file reads and tool calls proportionate, and stop polishing once it works. We start a new chat per task, and big features get split into sessions with a written prompt for each.

## Exercise

Ask: "You want three things today: fix a shop bug, design a new boss, and rename two pets. How do you split them into chats, and what settings does each get?"

Good answer: three chats (or rename and bug in one if they're tiny).
- Renames: small or middle model, low.
- Shop bug: middle, high.
- Boss design: biggest, high, starting with design questions.

## Apply it now

Offer to add a short "keep it simple" section to their CLAUDE.md if they don't have one.
